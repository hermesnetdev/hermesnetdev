Hosting without a public IP. Enidor turns the devices you already own into one private, peer-to-peer mesh. A drive, a service running on localhost, your phone's camera, the speakers on the machine in the next room — reach any of it from anywhere. No public IP, no port forwarding, no cloud in the middle.

What you can reach

If it runs on one of your devices, it's already hosted.

Pair once. From then on every drive, service and device in your ecosystem behaves like it's plugged into the machine in front of you.

No public IP. None needed.

No port forwarding, no static IP, no dynamic DNS, no VPN server to babysit. Devices find each other and hold the tunnel open themselves.

Storage, wherever it lives

Mount a paired device and browse its drive. Internal disks, an external SSD, a NAS folder or a project directory — all of it, without copying anything anywhere first.

Locally running services

A dev server on :3000, a media library, a home dashboard, a database, a model running on the desktop GPU. Reach a local port from another device as if it were local there too.

Hardware from your ecosystem

Your phone's camera and mic, the speakers on the machine in the next room, a scanner, a board on a workbench Pi. Devices you own become devices any of your devices can use.

Peer-to-peer, not round-tripped

Traffic goes straight from one device to the other, so your own network is the only speed limit. Nothing is staged in cloud storage on the way.

Stream instead of transfer

Video, audio and images play straight from the host over the tunnel, full screen included. Downloading is a choice, not a prerequisite.

Let someone else in, briefly

Hand out a 6-digit room code to give a guest exactly what you picked — up to five at a time. Revoke it with one click, or by quitting the app.

Updates itself

New versions download in the background and install themselves. You only ever download Enidor once per device.

Security & privacy

There’s no door to knock on.

The usual way to host something at home is to open a hole in your router and hope nobody finds it. Enidor doesn't open one at all — there's no public address to scan, and nothing in the middle that can read what passes through.

  • Nothing is exposed to the internet

    No open ports, no public hostname, no listener anyone can reach. Your devices dial out to each other instead of waiting to be found.

  • Paired devices only

    Every connection starts with an Ed25519 cryptographic handshake. Only devices you explicitly paired can open a tunnel or ask for anything through it.

  • A relay that can't see

    When strict NAT forces traffic through our relay, it's a dumb pipe. Everything is encrypted in transit, so the relay carries ciphertext and nothing else.

  • Mutual revocation

    Lost a device? Unpair it and the tunnel closes immediately. Trust is deleted on both ends and access is gone for good.

  • Strictly scoped access

    Only what you publish is reachable — the folders you mount, the ports you expose, the hardware you allow. The rest of the machine stays untouched.

Get started

Up and running in four steps.

  1. 01

    Install

    Get the build for your computer from the releases below, and install it on every device you want in your mesh.

  2. 02

    Pair your devices

    On the first device, click the phone icon in the sidebar to show a QR code. On the second, click + and scan it. Name the device and the mesh exists.

  3. 03

    Publish what you want to reach

    Under Mounted Vault Folders, click + to add a folder, a drive or a whole project. Paired devices can open it right away.

  4. 04

    Open it from anywhere

    Click any paired device that's online and it's just there. To bring in someone outside your mesh, click + Share New Folder and send them the 6-digit code.
